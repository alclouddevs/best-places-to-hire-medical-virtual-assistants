## How we selected and compared providers

We included providers whose official websites describe medical virtual assistance, healthcare staffing or a closely related remote administrative service. We reviewed the specific service positioning rather than assuming every general virtual assistant package is suitable for patient information.

The comparison considers six practical questions:

1. **Role fit:** Does the provider describe the work your practice needs?
2. **Candidate fit:** Can you evaluate specialty experience, language and software familiarity?
3. **Schedule fit:** Can the proposed person cover your exact clinic hours?
4. **Commercial clarity:** Are hours, rates, additional fees and replacement terms clear?
5. **Operational support:** Who handles onboarding, supervision and continuity?
6. **Patient-data arrangements:** What contracts, access controls and procedures apply to the engagement?

We have not assigned numerical ratings, audited security controls, verified every marketing claim or assessed actual candidates from all 12 companies. A useful shortlist must still be followed by interviews and contract review. MedicalVirtual is our first recommendation because its Latin American staffing model matches the US-hour and bilingual needs this guide prioritizes.

## Which role should you hire?

Define the work before choosing a provider. Hiring a general assistant for a specialist queue often creates more supervision than expected.

| Your main bottleneck | Role to discuss | Useful work sample | Practice responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Missed calls and scheduling backlog | Medical receptionist | Fictional patient call and appointment booking | Approved scripts, booking rules and escalation |
| Incomplete new-patient forms | Intake coordinator | Identify missing fields in a fictional intake packet | Required forms and exception handling |
| Eligibility checks before visits | Insurance verification specialist | Document a fictional benefits check | Verification standards and unresolved cases |
| Authorization backlog | Prior authorization specialist | Track a fictional request through an agreed checklist | Clinical documentation and escalation decisions |
| Claims and payment follow-up | Medical billing assistant | Explain a fictional denial work queue | Billing oversight and approval boundaries |
| Documentation backlog | Medical scribe | Draft a note from a fictional scenario | Clinician review and final documentation |
| Coding work | Medical coder | Specialty-appropriate coding exercise | Coding standards, review and credentials where required |
| Referral and record follow-up | Referral or records coordinator | Route a fictional request correctly | Release procedures and routing rules |

These are suggested hiring exercises, not promises that every listed provider offers every role. Use fictional or appropriately de-identified information during selection.

For a first hire, choose one queue with clear volume and ownership. For example, “cover incoming calls from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Eastern and complete approved scheduling tasks” is more useful than “help with everything.” If call volume fills the day, do not also budget the same hours for a full billing workload.

## How much does a medical virtual assistant cost?

Compare the same number of hours and the same scope. The starting rate is only one part of the buying decision.

### Hourly-rate examples

The following are arithmetic examples, not provider package quotes. The $12 and $15 rates are hypothetical comparison inputs.

| Hourly rate | 20 hours/week: weekly | 20 hours/week: average month | 40 hours/week: weekly | 40 hours/week: average month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $9.50 | $190 | $823.33 | $380 | $1,646.67 |
| $10 | $200 | $866.67 | $400 | $1,733.33 |
| $12 | $240 | $1,040 | $480 | $2,080 |
| $15 | $300 | $1,300 | $600 | $2,600 |

Average monthly cost = hourly rate × weekly hours × 52 ÷ 12. Actual invoices follow your contract and billable hours. Availability, minimum schedules, holidays and additional charges need separate confirmation.

### Monthly packages need a different calculation

A $1,999 package containing 160 hours has an effective included-hour price of about $12.49. A $1,299 package containing 80 hours is about $16.24 per included hour. These calculations describe Wishup's reviewed Prime package figures; they do not make those packages equivalent to another provider's service.

A 40-hour week averages about 173.33 hours per month across a year. A package with 160 included hours therefore needs an extra-hours discussion if you require that weekly schedule throughout the year.

### Ask for the total cost

- Recruitment, setup or onboarding fees, if any.
- Included and additional hours; overtime and holiday treatment.
- Minimum term, notice period and early cancellation charges.
- EHR, phone, messaging and other software seats.
- Equipment, secure access and technical support responsibilities.
- Training time and the practice manager's supervision time.
- Replacement terms and any transition cost.

When comparing with an in-office hire, use your own salary, payroll, benefits and overhead figures. Avoid applying an unsupported national savings percentage to your practice. Remote staff also cannot replace hands-on duties that must happen on site.

## How to evaluate privacy and security

Review the engagement with your privacy or compliance lead. A marketing badge or training certificate does not describe the complete operating arrangement.

HHS explains that business associate contracts set permitted uses and disclosures, safeguards, reporting responsibilities and other obligations. Establish the applicable relationship and documentation before patient information is shared. See [HHS business associate contract guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/sample-business-associate-agreement-provisions/index.html).

Use these questions in provider evaluation:

| Area | Ask the provider | Ask your own team |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contracting | Which entities sign, and what patient-data terms apply? | Who approves the agreement and scope? |
| Access | Which systems and permissions does the assistant need? | Can we provide individual accounts appropriate to the role? |
| Devices | What device controls, encryption and support apply? | What access method does our IT team approve? |
| Communication | Which phone, email and messaging channels are used? | Are those channels approved for the information involved? |
| Training | What training is completed and how is it documented? | What practice-specific procedures must we teach? |
| Incidents | Who reports concerns, through which channel and on what timeline? | Who receives and acts on those reports? |
| Oversight | Who reviews errors and handles replacement or removal? | Who owns daily quality review? |
| Offboarding | How are access, records and equipment handled at the end? | Who disables accounts and checks completion? |

If insurance is relevant to your decision, request current certificates and policy details and review them against the contract. Do not assume one party's policy covers another party's responsibilities.

## Interview questions and a practical work sample

### Ten questions for candidates

1. Which US specialties have you supported, and what did you personally do?
2. Which EHR and phone systems have you used regularly?
3. Walk through a normal day in your most similar role.
4. How would you handle a caller asking a clinical question outside your role?
5. What do you do when eligibility information is unclear?
6. How do you document an incomplete task so someone else can continue it?
7. How do you manage competing calls, messages and follow-up tasks?
8. What happens if your internet or workstation stops working?
9. What exact hours can you consistently cover, including relevant clock changes?
10. What would you need from us to start this role successfully?

For bilingual roles, test both languages in realistic administrative scenarios. Conversational fluency does not automatically demonstrate accurate medical scheduling or insurance communication.

### A 30-minute fictional work sample

Give each shortlisted candidate the same instructions and material:

- **10 minutes:** Role-play a patient scheduling call using a written booking policy.
- **10 minutes:** Complete the selected administrative task in a fictional record.
- **5 minutes:** Identify exceptions and explain who should handle them.
- **5 minutes:** Write a handoff note for the practice manager.

Assess accuracy, communication, adherence to instructions and judgment about escalation. A fluent candidate who invents missing information should not outscore a careful candidate who flags it. Choose an exercise relevant to the actual role; a receptionist does not need a specialist coding test.

## A practical 30-day onboarding plan

This is a suggested practice-led plan. The provider's actual onboarding service may differ.

| Period | Focus | Review before expanding work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before day one | Complete agreed contracts, access setup, scripts and ownership | Accounts work; responsibilities and escalation are clear |
| Days 1–5 | Observe and complete a small supervised queue | Sample accuracy and documentation; correct misunderstandings |
| Days 6–10 | Increase volume within the agreed role | Check turnaround, errors and the effect on existing staff |
| Days 11–20 | Stabilize routines and exception handling | Confirm unresolved work has an owner and a follow-up date |
| Days 21–30 | Compare performance with the starting baseline | Adjust workload, training and coverage before adding tasks |

### Measure work that matters

For reception, track calls answered, missed calls requiring follow-up, booking accuracy and message routing. For authorizations, track queue age, complete submissions and unresolved requests. For billing, review the agreed work queue and accuracy of documented follow-up rather than attributing every payment change to one hire.

Set targets from your own baseline and workload. Pair speed with a quality measure so people are not rewarded for closing tasks incorrectly. Review trends with the assistant and the practice owner of the workflow.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best place to hire a medical virtual assistant?

The best fit for a particular practice still depends on the candidate, duties, schedule, support and contract. Use the comparison to shortlist, then interview.

### What is a medical virtual assistant?

A remote professional who handles agreed healthcare administrative or documentation work through the practice's systems. Common roles include reception, intake, insurance support, records and scribing. The job title alone does not establish qualifications or authorize clinical duties.

### Is a medical virtual assistant the same as an in-office medical assistant?

No. A remote administrative worker cannot perform physical tasks such as taking vitals or preparing an examination room. Define which duties can be done remotely and which remain with on-site or licensed staff.

### Can a virtual assistant answer patient calls and book appointments?

Yes, if that is within the agreed role and the practice provides suitable systems, booking rules and scripts. Establish how urgent, unusual or clinical questions are escalated rather than leaving the assistant to improvise.

### Can they work in our EHR or EMR?

Potentially, subject to your system's access rules and your approved setup. Ask about the actual candidate's experience. Experience with one product does not establish competence in every configuration or specialty workflow.

### Can we hire someone bilingual in English and Spanish?

Yes. MedicalVirtual and Virtual Latinos both describe bilingual options. Other providers may also offer them. Confirm the availability and proficiency of the specific candidate rather than assuming bilingual support is included in every placement.

### Do assistants based in the Philippines work US hours?

Yes. Hello Rache explicitly recruits Philippines-based professionals for night shifts serving US clients. US daytime work generally falls during evening or overnight hours in the Philippines. Confirm the exact shift and continuity arrangements. Latin American locations can offer closer local daytime overlap with US clinics; exact overlap still depends on location and schedule.

### Can we hire part time?

Availability and minimum hours differ by provider and role. Wishup's reviewed guide lists an 80-hour Prime plan; that is not necessarily the same as 20 hours every week. Ask each provider to quote the precise schedule you need.

### How quickly can someone start?

Separate candidate matching from operational readiness. Even after you choose someone, contracts, system access and practice-specific onboarding must be ready. Agree a realistic start date for the role rather than treating a headline matching time as a guaranteed working start.

### Can one assistant handle reception, billing and scribing?

Some candidates have several skills, but time and simultaneous duties still matter. Someone answering live calls cannot also be continuously available to scribe a visit. Define priorities, expected volume and specialist competence before combining roles.

### How should we check references and experience?

Ask the provider what it verifies and what evidence it can share appropriately. Then use role-specific interview questions and a fictional exercise. Avoid requesting confidential patient records or an applicant's former employer's proprietary material.

### Who manages the assistant day to day?

It depends on the engagement. Recruitment, contracting, payroll administration, coaching and daily workflow supervision are different services. Establish a named owner for each; do not assume an agency fee means every management task is included.

### What happens if the assistant is not a good fit?

Review the written replacement and cancellation terms before hiring. Ask how a new shortlist is built, whether fees apply, what transition help is offered and how access is removed. A replacement promise should be clear enough to use in practice.

### How do we choose between two similar providers?

Use the same brief, compare actual candidates, run the same work sample and normalize the costs to the same hours. Then compare support, responsibilities and contract terms. The better decision is the arrangement that fits your workload, not necessarily the largest company or lowest advertised rate.
